🐄 Use of antibiotics in farming ‘endangering human immune system’
A new study has suggested that the widespread use of antibiotics in farming has led to the emergence of bacteria that are more resistant to the human immune system. The antimicrobial colistin, previously used as a growth promoter on chicken and pig farms, resulted in the emergence of E.coli strains that are more capable of evading the immune system's first line of defence. Although colistin is now banned as a livestock food additive in China and many other countries, the findings highlight the potential danger of the indiscriminate use of antimicrobials in agriculture.
